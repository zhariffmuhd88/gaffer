# GAFFER 26/27

A retro, CM 01/02-inspired football management game with real Premier League, Championship and League One 2026/27 squads, plus reserve (U21) and academy (U18) sides with their own youth leagues. It runs in the browser as a single HTML file and saves to browser storage. Clubs have drawn stadiums, board requests and building work, and a History section keeps real past winners, club honours and manager world rankings. An International section adds 142 nations, the Nations League, Euro 2028 and World Cup 2030 qualifying and finals, friendlies and caps, with England, Spain or Italy playable as a full-time or dual (club + country) job.

Spain is the second country: all 20 La Liga clubs with real squads (as of 31 Oct 2026), the Segunda División (team level, top two plus play-off winner promoted), the Copa del Rey (lower-division sides host single-game ties, two-legged semi-finals) and the four-team Supercopa (Istanbul, February 2027). La Liga ties are split by head-to-head record first. Spanish clubs play in their real 2026/27 European places, win La Liga awards (Player/Manager/Goal of the Month, Pichichi, Zamora, Team of the Season) and can trade players with English clubs. You can start in England or Spain and move between them.

The Bundesliga, Ligue 1 and Saudi Pro League (18 clubs each) are in the game as top flights only, with real first-team squads and head coaches from FotMob (6 Nov 2026); ratings are game estimates (club level, age curve and hand-set ratings for well-known players). There are no lower divisions and no relegation in these three leagues. German and French clubs play in their places in the game's 2026/27 European draw and qualify through their league position afterwards (top four Champions League, fifth Europa League, sixth Conference League); Saudi clubs enter the Club World Cup in place of their team-level versions. They have youth leagues, monthly and season awards, title and Fair Play announcements, and their players can be scouted and signed; stars leaving relegated clubs and players sold "abroad" now often join these clubs. Older saves get the three leagues on load (this season's results so far are simulated), and players earlier sold to Bayern, Paris Saint-Germain, Al-Hilal and the other new clubs join them.

League One (24 clubs) has real squads and head coaches from FotMob (12 Nov 2026; a few clubs' FotMob pages listed no ages, so those ages are estimates) and plays on the Championship's dates. The top two go up with the play-off winner (play-offs simulated), the bottom three of the Championship come down with their squads, and four clubs drop to League Two, which stays at team level (four League Two sides come up with generated squads). League One clubs enter the FA Cup; a manager relegated to League Two is dismissed.

The Eredivisie (18 clubs, top flight only, no relegation) has real squads and head coaches from FotMob (12 Nov 2026), its own youth leagues, the KNVB Cup (18-team knockout, final at De Kuip) and European places (top two to the Champions League, third to the Europa League, fourth to the Conference League). PSV, Ajax, Feyenoord, AZ, Twente and Utrecht play their 2026/27 European campaigns with full squads. The Netherlands can be managed like England, Spain and Italy (head coach Xavi Hernández, appointed in August 2026); Dutch caps and goals are game estimates. Older saves get League One, the Eredivisie, the KNVB Cup and the Dutch player pool added, with this season's results so far filled in at team level.

Manager options (Manager menu): every club manager has a job security rating from very secure to very unsecure, based on league position against the board's target, recent form and how patient that club's board and fans are (big clubs tend to be impatient). Very unsecure managers get sacked, and the club has a caretaker for a few weeks. In the job centre you can apply for vacant jobs and declare interest in jobs whose manager is under pressure; your own board, fans and players react (worst for a rival, milder for a clear step up). Clubs answer applications with a job offer or a rejection, and a club you showed interest in calls you first if it sacks its manager. Your own job follows the same rules: an impatient board reacts more to each result and sacks sooner, and very unhappy fans add pressure.

Italy is the third country: all 20 Serie A clubs with real squads (FotMob, 4 Nov 2026), Serie B (team level: top two promoted plus a play-off winner; bottom three plus the play-out loser drop to Serie C), the Coppa Italia (Serie A clubs outside last season's top eight and all of Serie B start in the first round, the top eight join in the round of 16, two-legged semi-finals, final at the Stadio Olimpico) and the one-match Supercoppa Italiana (Inter v Lazio at San Siro, 22 Dec 2026; afterwards champions v cup winners). Serie A ties are split by head-to-head record first. Italian clubs play in their real 2026/27 European places (Inter, Napoli, Roma and Como in the Champions League; Milan and Juventus in the Europa League; Atalanta in the Conference League), win Serie A awards (capocannoniere, Player/Manager/Goal of the Month, Team of the Season) and have Primavera and Under-18 youth leagues. Italy (coach Roberto Mancini) is playable, with caps from Wikipedia (5 Oct 2026) and the all-time caps and goals records. History adds Italian champions since 1929/30, the Coppa Italia and the Supercoppa. Older saves get Italy on load: this season's Serie A table up to the load date is filled in with simulated results.

On the Tactics screen you can pick the best XI, rotate the squad (a completely new XI: everyone in the current lineup is rested, fit or tired, and the best reserve or academy players fill any gaps), field a youth XI (players aged 21 or under, including reserves and academy, who may now start) and save up to eight named tactics with their lineup. Tap a shirt to move that player up, down, left or right (his position changes when he crosses into another zone, giving a custom shape such as 4-3-3*) or to swap him with a team-mate or a bench player.

A Reports screen (after Staff) holds the assistant manager's weekly report: who deserves to start and whom he would start them ahead of, who should be dropped or rested, the best form, who is underperforming and whom to sell in the next window (a better assistant judges more accurately). It also holds monthly scout reports: three scouts (the chief scout and two regional scouts) can each be assigned to a league (Premier League, Championship, La Liga, Serie A) or a region (South America, West Africa, Scandinavia, Iberia, Eastern Europe, UK & Ireland), with a brief (win-now signing, stop-gap, high-potential youngster or wonderkid) and a position, and recommend players with fee, scouted potential and a note. A player's profile now lists what he is happy about and what bothers him: the league position, Champions League football, the captaincy (proud to be captain, rates the captain, or feels he should have the armband), team-mates (a world-class team-mate, a friend, a training-ground row, a rival keeping him out of the team, being underpaid next to someone), the manager (his last chat, broken or pending promises), form, contract and the team's results.

Relegated clubs' stars are now sold to top-flight clubs in England, Spain or Italy where possible; players who still go abroad (outside the game's leagues) can be found in Transfers › Search with League "Abroad" and signed from their profile while a window is open.

Compare players (FC style): press Compare on any player's profile, or use the button on the Squad and Transfers screens, and add up to three players from anywhere (search by name: any club, youth, abroad, free agents). The screen shows card headers (overall, position, age, nation, club, value, wage, scouted potential), a hexagon radar of face stats derived from the game's attributes (PAC, SHO, PAS, DRI, DEF, PHY; DIV, HAN, KIC, REF, SPD, POS for goalkeepers), and side-by-side tables of face stats, season stats with per-90 numbers, form, happiness and every attribute, with the better value highlighted.

Penalty shoot-outs in a match you watch are played kick by kick: pick each taker and where he aims, and choose which way your keeper dives (quick sims and skipped matches resolve them automatically). For penalties during a match, everyone except the taker and the defending keeper lines up outside the box and the D. The social network in the game is WUPH.com (fictional); official and verified accounts (clubs, reporters, pundits, creators, former referees) carry a ✅. The intro includes a "Kick racism out of football" card. A tribute marks the death of CD Tenerife striker Jesús de Miguel (2 to 3 Nov 2026, as reported by the press): news, club and fan posts, a minute's applause before your next match, and a message against the online racist abuse that followed; no statements are attributed to real people or governing bodies.

Cup Winners' Cup entry (game scenario, played after the last league day): the season's FA Cup, League Cup, Copa del Rey and Coppa Italia winners; in a save that missed the previous edition, last season's cup winners are invited as well. If doubles leave fewer than 8 clubs, the cup runners-up come in, then each country's Fair Play Award winner, then other European clubs; more than 8 means a preliminary round with byes for the strongest. The Fair Play Award goes each season to the club with the fewest disciplinary points (yellow 1, red 3) in the Premier League, Championship, La Liga and Serie A.

As soon as a league title (Premier League, Championship, La Liga, Serie A) is mathematically secured, it is announced in the news, on WUPH.com (club, league, reporter, pundit and fan posts, trending tags) and in your inbox; winning it lifts your players' morale, the board and the fans, and a runner-up gets a "title race over" message.

The WUPH.com screen (formerly Media) adds the emotional side: fans react to results, derbies, signings, sales and trophies; a fan mood meter and trending hashtags; transfer rumours from fictional reporters with reliability tiers (Tier 1 usually right, Tier 3 mostly gossip) that can turn into real bids and transfers; insider "done deal" posts; club news; board-pressure stories. Fictional TV pundits argue over each big game, fan-channel creators melt down or celebrate, fans blame referees, VAR and the opposition's tactics in reply threads with rival fans, and every Monday a VAR Watch has fictional former referees judge the week's biggest calls (correct, wrong or debatable). Match incidents (disallowed goals, penalties, red cards) are recorded on each fixture.

Storyline (game scenario, not a real ruling; can be switched off in Game > Options until it lands): after their appeal, Manchester City are deducted 60 points on 29 Jan 2027 and in each of the next four seasons, and fined £500m.

Pre-season (from 1 July; the first season now starts on 1 July 2026): arrange friendlies yourself, let the assistant do it, or book an Asia Tour (Selangor, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, China) against real squads of Selangor FC, Yokohama F. Marinos, Lion City Sailors, Kitchee and Shanghai Port (FotMob and other sources, 8 Nov 2026; ratings are estimates) for appearance fees, reputation and commercial income. Every save plays Arsenal Legends each summer (prime-years ratings, game estimates; co-managers Arsène Wenger and Mikel Arteta; first XI in the first half, eleven substitutes in the second; the gold trophy is on show). Tour players can be viewed, compared and signed; legends can be viewed and compared but not signed. Friendlies do not count towards stats, cards or form.

WUPH.com banter includes rival fans' digs at each club's sore points and the comebacks to them (counts come from real honours plus your save), Judas and "finished" posts, hate-watching, ex-players facing their old clubs and Gen Z slang; fictional pundits have former clubs. Trophy wins and headline transfers get long news write-ups. Big signings sell shirts, and you choose a shirt sponsor (fictional brands) with perks and clauses. Unbeaten Premier League champions get the gold trophy (Arsenal 2003/04 in the honours).

Navigation is grouped (Home, Squad, Competitions, Club, World, Manager): collapsible on desktop, two compact rows on a phone. The inbox has filters (Important, Transfers, Staff, Media, All), "Mark all read", and one weekly Staff digest that collects the assistant's report, scout reports, fitness, morale and development notes. The WUPH.com badge counts only unread official, reporter and pundit posts about your club. First-week tips (tactics, assistant report, scouts, pre-season) can be dismissed.

Calibration (10-season Node runs, `node tests/calib.js 10 BRE`): a side three goals up eases off (two in internationals), underdogs raise their game in early cup rounds, a small home advantage, per-league scoring factors, and Como's ratings toned down.

3D matchday: a "3D" option next to "2D pitch" draws the same live scene with three.js r128 (loaded from cdnjs when chosen; needs an internet connection, otherwise the 2D pitch stays). A raked stadium with roofs, floodlights (glowing at night), advertising boards, dugouts and corner flags; crowds in club colours with an away end, thinner when the ground is not full; day or night and rain or snow from the fixture; players in their kits with shirt numbers, distinct goalkeeper kits, running, celebrating and diving; TV, wide and behind-the-goal cameras, a slow orbit round the scorer and a low, close camera for replays; the 2D labels (Penalty!, saves, REPLAY) shown over the picture; a Low quality toggle (no shadows, lower resolution; default on phones). `tests/md3d_real.py OUT THREE_JS` renders real screenshots when given a local three.min.js; `tests/md3d_browser.py` checks the wiring without it.

Cinematic intro: a 31-second film rendered in the game's 3D engine opens the game (skippable; "Tap for sound" for the synthesised soundtrack): a night fly-over of a bowl stadium inspired by a famous North London ground (no names or logos; also Arsenal's home ground in the 3D view), the walk-out, title cards, a dribble, a low-angle goal with the net rippling, a corner celebration with flares and confetti, and the final title. Game > Options turns it on or off, plays it again, and lets you add your own "Intro photo" with a caption: the photo is kept in this browser only (IndexedDB) and never uploaded or published. Without an internet connection the 2D intro cards play instead.

Manager statue: reaching legend status at a club (a league title and five seasons, or three major trophies) unveils an illustrated bronze statue outside the ground: the head is your own manager avatar (hair, beard, glasses) cast in bronze, the pose follows your honours (trophy aloft, fist pump or pointing), and a plaque gives your name, years, trophies and games. Eight trophies at the club add a gold laurel and "Club legend". The statue appears on the day (with the club-coloured cloth sliding off), on the club's honours page and on your manager profile.

Former players on the staff: Staff lists up to ten retired players and veterans in their last contract year (your own 33+ players included) who would start a new career as First-Team Coach, Assistant Manager, Chief Scout or Head Physio. Each has a non-playing profile (also reachable from his player card): ability for every role, coaching badges, medical training, speciality by position, scouting contacts, personality and his playing career in the save. Hiring one ends his playing career (a club still holding his contract asks a release fee); well-known players sometimes retire into a staff job at their last club on their own.

National-team trophies (World Cup, European Championship, Nations League) get a full celebration: a long news story (final score, how many titles and since when, top scorer, your club's players among the winners), a victory parade through the capital with a drawn parade scene, a drawn trophy banner in the inbox, a popup when it is your nation, "International honours" on the winners' player cards, a national honour and trophy banners on the manager profile if you managed them, and a trophy cabinet on International › History. Trophy drawings are generic shapes, not the real designs.

## Layout

- `src/`: game source (data, match engine, season, transfers, UI, 2D matchday, awards, manager profile, styles)
- `build.py`: joins `src/` into `gaffer.html` (and `bundle.js` for tests)
- `gaffer.html`: the latest built game
- `tests/`: Node simulation tests and Playwright smoke tests (run from the repo root after building), e.g. `node tests/ch_test.js WHU 2`
- `tools/build_ch.py`: builds `src/data_ch.js` (Championship squads) from the raw lists in `tools/ch_raw/` (FotMob team pages, 24-28 Oct 2026); ratings are game estimates
- `tools/build_youth.py`: builds `src/data_youth.js` (real reserve and academy players for 28 clubs) from `tools/youth_raw/` (FotMob U21 team pages, 28-29 Oct 2026); clubs without a published list get generated youth players marked GEN
- `tools/build_hist.py`: builds `src/data_hist.js` (real winners and runners-up to 2025/26) from `tools/hist_raw/` (Wikipedia list pages, read 30 Oct 2026)
- `src/data_stadia.js`: stadium facts (opening year, record crowd, pitch size, stand names) from Wikipedia stadium pages; `src/data_mgr.js`: managers and major honours for the world rankings
- `tools/build_ll.py`: builds `src/data_ll.js` (La Liga squads, Segunda and Primera Federación sides, Spanish stadium facts, Spain caps) from `tools/ll_raw/` (FotMob team pages, 31 Oct 2026; coaches, captains and grounds from Wikipedia "2026-27 La Liga"); ages, positions and ratings are game estimates, Spanish reserve and academy players are generated (GEN)
- `src/selection.js`: Best XI / Rotate / Youth XI and saved tactics on the Tactics screen
- `src/social.js`: Media screen (fans, fictional reporters, rumours, trending)
- `src/clublife.js`: player requests with answers, documentary, revived Cup Winners' Cup (game scenario) and Club World Cup (game format), trophy room, trophy parade, statue, manager's son, save export/import as a file
- `src/banter.js`: pundits, creators, blame and banter threads, VAR Watch
- `src/scenario.js`: optional storylines (Manchester City sanction)
- `src/laliga.js`: Spanish competitions, Copa del Rey and Supercopa draws, season end, migration that adds Spain to older saves
- `src/data_intl.js`: nations (FIFA ranking, Oct 2026), Nations League 2026/27 draw and results, England caps, international winners; checked 31 Oct 2026
- `src/rivalry.js`, `src/preseason.js`, `src/polish.js`: rivalry banter, sponsors and news write-ups; pre-season, Asia Tour and Arsenal Legends; grouped navigation, inbox digest and filters, tips, cup and calibration tweaks
- `tests/run_all.sh [OUT_DIR] [JOBS]`: builds and runs every node and browser test, prints ok/FAIL per test; `tests/calib.js`: 10-season calibration run
- `docs/screenshots/`: reference screenshots

## Build

```bash
python3 build.py      # writes gaffer.html + bundle.js
python3 build_pwa.py  # writes the web app: index.html, manifest, sw.js, icons/
```

Saves are compressed in browser storage (LZW, about a tenth of the JSON size); older uncompressed saves still load. Export backup gives plain JSON.

`src/vis.js` is an older draft of the 2D view and is not part of the build.
