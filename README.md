# GAFFER 26/27

A retro, CM 01/02-inspired football management game with real Premier League, Championship and League One 2026/27 squads, plus reserve (U21) and academy (U18) sides with their own youth leagues. It runs in the browser as a single HTML file and saves to browser storage. Clubs have drawn stadiums, board requests and building work, and a History section keeps real past winners, club honours and manager world rankings. An International section adds 142 nations, the Nations League, Euro 2028 and World Cup 2030 qualifying and finals, friendlies and caps, with England, Spain or Italy playable as a full-time or dual (club + country) job.

Spain is the second country: all 20 La Liga clubs with real squads (as of 31 Oct 2026), the Segunda División (team level, top two plus play-off winner promoted), the Copa del Rey (lower-division sides host single-game ties, two-legged semi-finals) and the four-team Supercopa (Istanbul, February 2027). La Liga ties are split by head-to-head record first. Spanish clubs play in their real 2026/27 European places, win La Liga awards (Player/Manager/Goal of the Month, Pichichi, Zamora, Team of the Season) and can trade players with English clubs. You can start in England or Spain and move between them.

The Bundesliga, Ligue 1 and Saudi Pro League (18 clubs each) are in the game as top flights only, with real first-team squads and head coaches from FotMob (6 Nov 2026); ratings are game estimates (club level, age curve and hand-set ratings for well-known players). There are no lower divisions and no relegation in these three leagues. German and French clubs play in their places in the game's 2026/27 European draw and qualify through their league position afterwards (top four Champions League, fifth Europa League, sixth Conference League); Saudi clubs enter the Club World Cup in place of their team-level versions. They have youth leagues, monthly and season awards, title and Fair Play announcements, and their players can be scouted and signed; stars leaving relegated clubs and players sold "abroad" now often join these clubs. Older saves get the three leagues on load (this season's results so far are simulated), and players earlier sold to Bayern, Paris Saint-Germain, Al-Hilal and the other new clubs join them.

League One (24 clubs) has real squads and head coaches from FotMob (12 Nov 2026; a few clubs' FotMob pages listed no ages, so those ages are estimates) and plays on the Championship's dates. The top two go up with the play-off winner (play-offs simulated), the bottom three of the Championship come down with their squads, and four clubs drop to League Two, which stays at team level (four League Two sides come up with generated squads). League One clubs enter the FA Cup; a manager relegated to League Two is dismissed.

The Eredivisie (18 clubs, top flight only, no relegation) has real squads and head coaches from FotMob (12 Nov 2026), its own youth leagues, the KNVB Cup (18-team knockout, final at De Kuip) and European places (top two to the Champions League, third to the Europa League, fourth to the Conference League). PSV, Ajax, Feyenoord, AZ, Twente and Utrecht play their 2026/27 European campaigns with full squads. The Netherlands can be managed like England, Spain and Italy (head coach Xavi Hernández, appointed in August 2026); Dutch caps and goals are game estimates. Older saves get League One, the Eredivisie, the KNVB Cup and the Dutch player pool added, with this season's results so far filled in at team level.

Manager options (Manager menu): every club manager has a job security rating from very secure to very unsecure, based on league position against the board's target, recent form and how patient that club's board and fans are (big clubs tend to be impatient). Very unsecure managers get sacked, and the club has a caretaker for a few weeks. In the job centre you can apply for vacant jobs and declare interest in jobs whose manager is under pressure; your own board, fans and players react (worst for a rival, milder for a clear step up). Clubs answer applications with a job offer or a rejection, and a club you showed interest in calls you first if it sacks its manager. Your own job follows the same rules: an impatient board reacts more to each result and sacks sooner, and very unhappy fans add pressure.

Players at computer-run clubs have detailed happiness too: each week their mood follows concrete reasons (game time against their status, pride or embarrassment at the league position against the club's target, a title race or a relegation fight, faith in the manager or excitement about a new one, form, contract, wages, having outgrown the club, captaincy, Champions League nights, homesickness). Their player card shows "Happy about" and "Bothered by" lists like your own players' cards, the Compare screen shows their happiness, a big name falling out of love makes the news, and unsettled players are cheaper to sign.

Player and club stories: every player card has a season-by-season history from 2026/27 (club, appearances, goals, assists, clean sheets for goalkeepers) and a "Likes and dislikes" section (favoured clubs and people, least favoured). Club pages (and the Club lore tab on your own Club screen) show rivals, club legends and fans' villains: real-world fan sentiment with the reason (for example Arsenal supporters' feelings about Robin van Persie's 2012 move to Manchester United), plus legends made in the save (150 games for a club, or a manager with three trophies) and players who joined a rival directly. Manager names are clickable: nationality, honours, playing and managing careers before the save (a game summary of public records to 2025/26 for well-known managers), jobs, league finishes and trophies in the save, and their favoured clubs and people.

Football politics (game scenario): the world governing body is the fictional World Football Federation (WFF), with fictional officials. In 2026/27 it consults on the daylight offside law (an idea first put forward publicly by Arsène Wenger), a challenger runs for president on it, wins the May 2027 congress vote and the law comes in for 2027/28 (attacking play gets a small boost). You can back a candidate. Then a scandal: leaked documents reveal the former president's plan to sell off the 2026 World Cup's commercial rights; he is suspended, found guilty by the ethics committee in March 2028 (eight-year ban) and loses his appeal. Saves that are already past these dates get a summary of what happened. The WFF page (World menu) shows the officials, the election and the laws of the game, with real-world laws marked as such.

Behind the Badge episodes can be opened: title, summary, the episode scene by scene from real results, table and injuries, a talking point and quotes. Quotes come only from fictional people (you, your staff, invented crew, generated academy players, the narrator); real players appear in the narration.

Ballon d'Or with managers voting (game scenario): a 30-man shortlist from the season's performances is published when the season ends; you rank your top five, every club manager votes (with a slight lean to his own players, compatriots and league) and the winner is crowned in late September.

Italy is the third country: all 20 Serie A clubs with real squads (FotMob, 4 Nov 2026), Serie B (team level: top two promoted plus a play-off winner; bottom three plus the play-out loser drop to Serie C), the Coppa Italia (Serie A clubs outside last season's top eight and all of Serie B start in the first round, the top eight join in the round of 16, two-legged semi-finals, final at the Stadio Olimpico) and the one-match Supercoppa Italiana (Inter v Lazio at San Siro, 22 Dec 2026; afterwards champions v cup winners). Serie A ties are split by head-to-head record first. Italian clubs play in their real 2026/27 European places (Inter, Napoli, Roma and Como in the Champions League; Milan and Juventus in the Europa League; Atalanta in the Conference League), win Serie A awards (capocannoniere, Player/Manager/Goal of the Month, Team of the Season) and have Primavera and Under-18 youth leagues. Italy (coach Roberto Mancini) is playable, with caps from Wikipedia (5 Oct 2026) and the all-time caps and goals records. History adds Italian champions since 1929/30, the Coppa Italia and the Supercoppa. Older saves get Italy on load: this season's Serie A table up to the load date is filled in with simulated results.

On the Tactics screen you can pick the best XI, rotate the squad (a completely new XI: everyone in the current lineup is rested, fit or tired, and the best reserve or academy players fill any gaps), field a youth XI (players aged 21 or under, including reserves and academy, who may now start) and save up to eight named tactics with their lineup. Tap a shirt to move that player up, down, left or right (his position changes when he crosses into another zone, giving a custom shape such as 4-3-3*) or to swap him with a team-mate or a bench player.

A Reports screen (after Staff) holds the assistant manager's weekly report: who deserves to start and whom he would start them ahead of, who should be dropped or rested, the best form, who is underperforming and whom to sell in the next window (a better assistant judges more accurately). It also holds monthly scout reports: three scouts (the chief scout and two regional scouts) can each be assigned to a league (Premier League, Championship, La Liga, Serie A) or a region (South America, West Africa, Scandinavia, Iberia, Eastern Europe, UK & Ireland), with a brief (win-now signing, stop-gap, high-potential youngster or wonderkid) and a position, and recommend players with fee, scouted potential and a note. A player's profile now lists what he is happy about and what bothers him: the league position, Champions League football, the captaincy (proud to be captain, rates the captain, or feels he should have the armband), team-mates (a world-class team-mate, a friend, a training-ground row, a rival keeping him out of the team, being underpaid next to someone), the manager (his last chat, broken or pending promises), form, contract and the team's results.

Relegated clubs' stars are now sold to top-flight clubs in England, Spain or Italy where possible; players who still go abroad (outside the game's leagues) can be found in Transfers › Search with League "Abroad" and signed from their profile while a window is open.

Compare players (FC style): press Compare on any player's profile, or use the button on the Squad and Transfers screens, and add up to three players from anywhere (search by name: any club, youth, abroad, free agents). The screen shows card headers (overall, position, age, nation, club, value, wage, scouted potential), a hexagon radar of face stats derived from the game's attributes (PAC, SHO, PAS, DRI, DEF, PHY; DIV, HAN, KIC, REF, SPD, POS for goalkeepers), and side-by-side tables of face stats, season stats with per-90 numbers, form, happiness and every attribute, with the better value highlighted.

Penalty shoot-outs in a match you watch are played kick by kick: pick each taker and where he aims, and choose which way your keeper dives (quick sims and skipped matches resolve them automatically). For penalties during a match, everyone except the taker and the defending keeper lines up outside the box and the D. The social network in the game is WUPH.com (fictional); official and verified accounts (clubs, reporters, pundits, creators, former referees) carry a ✅. The intro includes a "Kick racism out of football" card. A tribute marks the death of CD Tenerife striker Jesús de Miguel (2 to 3 Nov 2026, as reported by the press): news, club and fan posts, a minute's applause before your next match, and a message against the online racist abuse that followed; no statements are attributed to real people or governing bodies.

Cup Winners' Cup entry (game scenario, played after the last league day): the season's FA Cup, League Cup, Copa del Rey and Coppa Italia winners; in a save that missed the previous edition, last season's cup winners are invited as well. If doubles leave fewer than 8 clubs, the cup runners-up come in, then each country's Fair Play Award winner, then other European clubs; more than 8 means a preliminary round with byes for the strongest. The Fair Play Award goes each season to the club with the fewest disciplinary points (yellow 1, red 3) in the Premier League, Championship, La Liga and Serie A.

As soon as a league title (Premier League, Championship, La Liga, Serie A) is mathematically secured, it is announced in the news, on WUPH.com (club, league, reporter, pundit and fan posts, trending tags) and in your inbox; winning it lifts your players' morale, the board and the fans, and a runner-up gets a "title race over" message.

The WUPH.com screen (formerly Media) adds the emotional side: fans react to results, derbies, signings, sales and trophies; a fan mood meter and trending hashtags; transfer rumours from fictional reporters with reliability tiers (Tier 1 usually right, Tier 3 mostly gossip) that can turn into real bids and transfers; insider "done deal" posts; club news; board-pressure stories. Fictional TV pundits argue over each big game, fan-channel creators melt down or celebrate, fans blame referees, VAR and the opposition's tactics in reply threads with rival fans, and every Monday a VAR Watch has fictional former referees judge the week's biggest calls (correct, wrong or debatable). Match incidents (disallowed goals, penalties, red cards) are recorded on each fixture.

Storyline (game scenario, not a real ruling; can be switched off in Game > Options until it lands): after their appeal, Manchester City are deducted 60 points on 29 Jan 2027 and in each of the next four seasons, and fined £500m.

Pre-season (from 1 July; the first season now starts on 1 July 2026): arrange friendlies yourself, let the assistant do it, or book an Asia Tour (Selangor, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, China) against real squads of Selangor FC, Yokohama F. Marinos, Lion City Sailors, Kitchee and Shanghai Port (FotMob and other sources, 8 Nov 2026; ratings are estimates) for appearance fees, reputation and commercial income. Every save plays Arsenal Legends each summer (prime-years ratings, game estimates; co-managers Arsène Wenger and Mikel Arteta; first XI in the first half, eleven substitutes in the second; the gold trophy is on show). Tour players can be viewed, compared and signed; legends can be viewed and compared but not signed. Friendlies do not count towards stats, cards or form.

WUPH.com banter includes rival fans' digs at each club's sore points and the comebacks to them (counts come from real honours plus your save), Judas and "finished" posts, hate-watching, ex-players facing their old clubs and Gen Z slang; fictional pundits have former clubs. Trophy wins and headline transfers get long news write-ups. Big signings sell shirts, and you choose a shirt sponsor (fictional brands) with perks and clauses. Unbeaten Premier League champions get the gold trophy (Arsenal 2003/04 in the honours).

Navigation is grouped (Home, Squad, Competitions, Club, World, Manager): collapsible on desktop, two compact rows on a phone. The inbox has filters (Important, Transfers, Staff, Media, All), "Mark all read", and one weekly Staff digest that collects the assistant's report, scout reports, fitness, morale and development notes. The WUPH.com badge counts only unread official, reporter and pundit posts about your club. First-week tips (tactics, assistant report, scouts, pre-season) can be dismissed.

Calibration (10-season Node runs, `node tests/calib.js 10 BRE`): a side three goals up eases off (two in internationals), underdogs raise their game in early cup rounds, a small home advantage, per-league scoring factors, and Como's ratings toned down.

3D matchday: a "3D" option next to "2D pitch" draws the same live scene with three.js r128 (loaded from cdnjs when chosen; needs an internet connection, otherwise the 2D pitch stays). A raked stadium with roofs, floodlights (glowing at night), advertising boards, dugouts and corner flags; crowds in club colours with an away end, thinner when the ground is not full; day or night and rain or snow from the fixture; players in their kits with shirt numbers, distinct goalkeeper kits, running, celebrating and diving; TV, wide and behind-the-goal cameras, a slow orbit round the scorer and a low, close camera for replays; the 2D labels (Penalty!, saves, REPLAY) shown over the picture; a Low quality toggle (no shadows, lower resolution; default on phones). `tests/md3d_real.py OUT THREE_JS` renders real screenshots when given a local three.min.js; `tests/md3d_browser.py` checks the wiring without it.

3D matchday, Phase 3: players act out what the 2D plan says (a kick with the stronger foot on passes, crosses and shots; jumps for headers; slide tackles; the keeper catching, throwing or kicking; throw-ins) and run with a walk, jog or sprint cycle by speed; names on the back of the shirt, the captain's armband, collar and cuff trim in club colours. A referee and two assistants (flag up for offside), both managers pacing their technical areas (they jump when their side scores) and a fourth official with a substitution board. A score and clock box in the corner, a GOAL banner with the scorer, a big screen in the stadium showing the live score, and a short fly-over before kick-off (tap to skip). A panelled ball that spins the way it travels, a trail on hard shots, and the net ripples on goals. Club colours only; no logos or crests. Low quality skips the shot trail.

3D matchday, Phase 4: the crowd sways and the scoring side's stands jump on goals; flags wave in the crowd and fan banners hang on the front of the stands; a tifo at the home end before derbies; flares and smoke in the stands when a goal goes in (crowd atmosphere); confetti at the final whistle of a final and on the day the title is won. Mowing patterns vary by club (stripes, wide stripes, checks, diagonals), the goalmouths and centre circle wear as the season goes on, the pitch shines in the rain and snow settles along the edges; early kick-offs on clear days get golden-hour light, and night games four soft floodlight shadows per player. Camera: drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom, double-tap to reset; a Player cam that follows a player you pick; replays in slow motion from three angles (side-on, behind the goal, reverse). Low quality uses fewer flags, flares and confetti and no floodlight shadows.

3D matchday, Phase 5: every player has a stable look of his own, made from his id only (never from nationality or name): skin tone from a wide, even palette, hair style (short, buzz, bald, curly, long tied back, dreadlocks, fade), hair colour, beard and boot colour; the player pop-up has an Appearance editor (stored in the save) and a favourite goal celebration. Body shape follows attributes (strength, pace, heading, position). Kits carry well-known club patterns in club colours (stripes, hoops, halves, sash, centre band, contrasting sleeves; no logos); keepers wear gloves; long sleeves in cold or wet weather, tights and snoods in snow. Celebrations: knee slide, run to the fans, arms out, fist pump or leap, with team-mates piling on. Ceremonies: before kick-off the teams walk out of the tunnel and line up for the team photo (the clock waits; tap to skip); at full time the players shake hands along the halfway line and the winners applaud their fans. Low quality uses simpler hair and no faces.

3D matchday, Phase 6: players are now smooth, skinned human models with a skeleton (hips, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulders, upper and lower arms, hands, thighs, shins, feet), built in code (no third-party model files, so nothing to license). Kit colours and club patterns go on the shirt, sleeves, shorts and socks; skin tone, hair, beard and boots come from the player's look; body shape from attributes; number, name, crest and captain's armband ride on the bones. The existing pose system drives the bones, with knees and elbows bending naturally in runs, kicks, headers, tackles, dives, celebrations and ceremonies. Choosing Low quality by hand keeps the old block figures; on slow devices the game switches to them by itself.

3D matchday, Phase 7: smoother movement on the human models. Poses blend into each other instead of snapping; the running stride matches the ground covered, so feet do not slide; players lean into turns, take a touch of the ball every other stride when dribbling (arms out for balance), pass with the inside of the foot, and get up properly after a fall; heads turn to follow the ball; faces get a mouth. Referees, assistants (flag on the forearm), the fourth official and both managers use the new models too.

3D matchday, Phase 8: modern rendering. sRGB output with ACES filmic tone mapping and every colour and texture in linear space; on High quality, physically based (roughness) materials for players, ball, pitch and stands, soft shadows with a larger shadow map, grass with blade streaks, noise and a bump map, a wet pitch that really shines in the rain, and a soft bloom around floodlights (glow sprites rather than a post-process pass, so phones stay smooth). Low quality keeps the light materials; slow devices fall back by themselves.

3D matchday, Phase 9: a real 3D crowd. Thousands of fans (about 9,000 on desktop, 2,600 on phones) as instanced figures on every stand, filled by attendance, in club shirts, the second colour and everyday clothes, with the away fans in their end; their motion runs on the GPU: a slow sway, standing when their team attacks or the teams walk out, jumping on goals, the winners on their feet at full time. Near the pitch: stewards in hi-vis facing the crowd, photographers crouched behind the goals, open dugouts with padded seats and the substitutes sitting in them, and two substitutes per side warming up in bibs. Choosing Low quality by hand keeps only the painted crowd; slow devices drop the 3D crowd by themselves.

3D matchday, Phase 10: the stadium is built to fit the club. Grounds under 22,000 seats get low stands, open corners, floodlight pylons and a standing terrace with crush barriers at the home end; grounds up to 45,000 get a single tier all the way round; bigger grounds get two tiers with a glass band of boxes between them. Every stand has stairways, vomitories (entrances) and roof trusses. Pitchside: a covered tunnel, marked technical areas and goal stanchions. The advertising boards are LED panels that scroll generic messages and, if you uploaded one, your club badge.

Matchday, Phase 11: smarter player movement (2D and 3D). The result and events still come from the match engine; only how the players move changes. Forwards sit on the last defender's shoulder and never stray offside before the pass, and time their run for a through ball. Wingers hold the width, and the far-side winger tucks in. Full-backs overlap (inverted full-backs underlap) when the tactic allows. Midfielders arrive late at the edge of the box. Two team-mates form a triangle around the ball, with a safe pass back. On crosses and cut-backs there are near-post, far-post, penalty-spot and edge-of-the-box runs, and the striker peels off his marker. Out of possession the back line steps up and drops together (faster with the offside trap), the block shifts across to the ball, pressing has triggers (a heavy touch near the line, a pass being received, a back pass), a midfielder blocks the nearest pass and the keeper sweeps behind a high line. Pace, Stamina and condition set each player's speed, so tired players jog and arrive late, and an amber or red stamina arrow marks fading players. The game has no separate Off the Ball, Anticipation or Work Rate attributes; these are read from Creativity, Flair, Finishing, Positioning, Determination, Teamwork and Stamina. The Movement arrows button (2D and 3D) shows where your players are about to run.

3D matchday, Phase 12: photo mode, clips and automatic quality. 📷 Photo mode pauses the match and hides the graphics. You can move the camera by dragging or pinching, or with the Orbit, Higher, Lower and Zoom buttons. Pick a look (Broadcast, Film, Black & white or Vintage; film and vintage add grain and a vignette) and save a PNG. On iPhone and iPad it opens the share sheet. Each goal replay has a ⬇ Clip button: it plays the replay again and records the pitch (2D or 3D) as a video, MP4 where the browser supports it and WebM otherwise. Browsers that cannot record get a short message instead. The quality button now starts on Auto, which watches the frame time and steps down by itself: from High to Medium (no shadows, lower resolution, no glow), then to Low (no 3D crowd, simpler players). Choosing Low or High by hand always wins. Tests that need real WebGL use tests/vendor/three.min.js (three.js r128, MIT licence).

Matchday, Phase 13: sound, commentary and officials (2D and 3D). All stadium sound is made with Web Audio, with no sound files, and starts only after a tap (as iOS requires). The crowd swells when a team attacks, more for the home side, and groans or goes "ooh" at misses. A goal sounds different for home and away: a full roar, or the away end cheering while the home crowd groans. Now and then the crowd claps a pattern or hums a chant, sings "olé" during a long passing spell, and murmurs during a VAR check. A PA chime introduces text announcements: the welcome, substitutions and the attendance. One volume slider sits next to the Sound button. Commentary captions describe key moments with the players' names and varied wording, and can be switched off. VAR: goals the engine disallows get the full check — the VAR graphic, the referee's hand to his ear, a picture of the move with the offside line, then NO GOAL. Some goals that stand, penalties and red cards are checked too, always ending with the engine's decision, and some red cards go to the pitch-side monitor. Goal-line technology appears for goals after a scramble and for saves on the line. In 3D the referee raises the yellow or red card, and a sent-off player walks off. Officials and announcements are fictional and generic.

Matchday, Phase 14: set pieces and the human side (2D and 3D). Free kicks get a wall 9.15 m from the ball, with more men the closer the kick is to goal. The keeper lines up the wall and stands off to the open side, the taker steps back for his run-up, and the wall jumps as the ball is struck. Penalties: the taker runs up from behind the ball, some with a stutter, and the keeper dives the way the engine decided. Penalty shoot-outs are now shown on the pitch as well as in the panel: both teams stand in the centre circle, each taker walks to the spot, and the kick and the dive follow the engine's result. Corners: attackers and their markers jostle in the box and the taker raises an arm. Injuries: the player stays down while two physios run on, with a medical buggy for serious injuries, and he limps for a while afterwards. The fourth official holds up the added-time board at the end of each half. Substitutions: the player going off walks to the halfway line and high-fives his replacement, and the bench applauds a big name. On the touchline, managers appeal decisions, crouch at big chances and shout instructions, and the bench jumps up for a goal. The substitutes now really sit in the dugouts. Positions follow the engine's plan; only the picture changes.

Matchday, Phase 15: broadcast presentation, in the style of the pre-match line-up graphic. Half-time and full-time cards show the score, the scorers with minutes, a stats panel (possession, shots, on target, xG, corners, cards) and the top three ratings. At full time a second card shows the Player of the Match, with his shirt in club colours and kit pattern, his rating and key numbers. Tap to move on, or the cards move on by themselves; they can be switched off in Game. From the cards, either team's heatmap or pass network can be laid on the pitch (2D and 3D); a chip at the top clears it. During play there is a live stats ticker, and lower-thirds for cards and substitutions. In 3D the goal replay plays picture-in-picture while the game restarts on the big picture (tap the inset to skip it). After the match a pitch-side interview with a fictional reporter asks two questions, each with three answers. The first uses the existing interview effects; the second gives a small boost to the fans, the board or morale. There is no real broadcaster or competition branding; competition logos appear only where you uploaded them on this device.

Matchday, Phase 16: the big occasions, kits and conditions. After a won final (club or national team), or the game that clinches the league, there is a trophy ceremony. The winners line up in front of a podium and receive their medals one by one, and the captain lifts a generic trophy whose shape depends on the competition type (league, domestic cup, European cup, national team), with no real trophy replicas or logos. Confetti and fireworks follow, then the squad photo with the trophy, which Photo mode can save. Every club has home, away and third kits in its colours; the away side wears the first one that does not clash. The kit editor in Club > Overview sets the colours and pattern of all three of your kits and saves them with the game. During the match, late kick-offs go from sunset to floodlights, fog limits the far view on some winter days, wind bends the flags and corner flags, and heavy rain leaves puddles. By the pitch there are ball boys, a mascot holds the home captain's hand at the walk-out, and on summer days the sprinklers run before kick-off.

Relationships (Manager > Relationships): every manager and every player at another club has a relationship with you from -100 (feud) to +100 (close friends). Managers' relationships follow them when they change club. You build them by sending a message, inviting a manager to dinner, praising or criticising him in the media, asking a friendly manager for a tip-off, shaking hands (or not) after a game, an extra question about the opposing manager in the post-match interview, and a new press-conference answer that praises the opposing manager (mind games now cost you with him). With players at other clubs you can send a message, praise them in the media (his manager will not like it) or have a word at full time after playing his club, and players who leave you on good terms stay friends. Effects: a friendly manager asks up to 12% less for his players and passes on tip-offs about unhappy or loan-listed players, a feud raises the price and ends up in the papers, and players who like you are keener to join and ask for slightly lower wages. Real managers and players only appear in reported game events, never as quotes.

Offers for your players must be answered. Each offer has a reply-by date, shown in the inbox message: six days after it arrives, or the day before the transfer window closes if that comes first. The game stops on that day, and Continue opens a window listing every offer that needs an answer (accept, ask for 25% more, or reject). It cannot be closed by tapping outside it or pressing Escape, so an offer never runs out unanswered.

Cinematic intro, refreshed after Phases 3–16: the 31-second film now uses the human models (block figures on phones), with your club's kit pattern and varied looks. A mascot walks out holding the captain's hand, and broadcast lower-thirds appear (line-ups, a LIVE score bug, the goal). A new trophy-lift scene follows the celebration: a podium, the captain lifting the cup on the beat, the team jumping behind him, and fireworks and confetti. The memories shot shows the squad photo with the trophy when no personal photo is set. Every cut after the title falls on a bar line of the 128 bpm soundtrack, and the goal lands on the second impact. tests/cine_real.py now uses tests/vendor/three.min.js by default and holds the film at each moment for its screenshots (UI.cineAt).

Pre-match line-ups: a broadcast-style graphic for each team before kick-off (team name and formation, the XI on a pitch with shirts in club colours and pattern, numbers and surnames, substitutes and manager, a strip with the competition and its logo if you uploaded one). Tap to move on; it moves on by itself after a few seconds, can be opened again from the Line-ups button, and can be switched off in Game.

Squad depth (Reports › Assistant manager): a Football Manager-style depth chart for every position in your formation: the best three options with star ratings against the level of your first eleven (that level = 4 stars), injuries marked, and a verdict (Good, Adequate, Lacking quality, Lacking depth, Ageing, Long-term injury). Under "Where to strengthen" the assistant names up to three positions in order of need with a target rating, and Find players opens Player search filtered to that position. A better assistant judges more accurately. `tests/depth_browser.py` covers it.

Agreed fees survive the deadline: if a club has accepted your bid and the window closes before you agree personal terms, the deal stays in Transfers > Deals (and the inbox). Agree terms while the window is shut and the player joins on the first day of the next window, before other clubs can move; the deal falls through only if he has left that club or your transfer budget no longer covers the fee. `tests/deal_test.js` covers it.

Budget split: on the Transfers screen (and Club > Board) a slider moves money between the transfer budget and the weekly wage budget, as in Football Manager: £1m of transfers pays about £19.2k a week for a season. Wages can go up as far as the transfer budget allows and down to the current wage bill; confirm to apply.

Transfers > Player search: a "Would join you" filter (Keen to join / Would at least consider) that weighs club stature, division, his role at his club, how settled he is, whether he is listed or has asked to leave, and rivalries; the Status filter adds "Loan listed" (young players and surplus squad players outside their club's top 18 whom the club would loan out) and "Transfer or loan listed". Other clubs' player pop-ups show a transfer outlook (keen, would consider, not interested; listed; available on loan).

Career XP and rewards (Manager > Rewards): your manager career earns XP on this device across all your saves (win 10, draw 4, cup or European trophy 250, league title 400, promotion 300, full season 500; friendlies do not count; each match, trophy and season counts once even if you reload an older save). Levels run from Sunday League to Immortal. Milestones unlock rewards that can be redeemed once in every save: 1 season £20m transfer kitty; 2 seasons board and fans' trust; level 3 squad refresh; 3 seasons a wonderkid in the academy; 3 trophies a £100m sponsor windfall; 5 seasons £200m transfer budget; level 6 two-year contract extensions; 7 seasons sign any player from any club for free; 10 seasons two more free signings and £300m. Saves started before rewards existed count their finished seasons, trophies and this season's matches once, the first time they are loaded. `tests/rewards_test.js` covers it.

Your own club badges: Club > Overview > Club badge lets you upload a badge or logo for the club you manage, and every club's pop-up (tap a club's name; friendly opponents such as Arsenal Legends too) has the same option. It is stored in this browser on this device only (IndexedDB): it is not in the save or a save export and is never uploaded, committed or published, so the game ships no real club logos. Where a badge exists it replaces the colour swatch (tables, links, the match header) and shows in the 3D score box, on the big screen and on the shirts. Competition logos work the same way (device-only): Game › Competition logos lists every league, cup and tournament, and the league and cup pages have a quick "Add logo" button; logos show in match headers, fixtures, the league and cup pages and the 3D score box. `tests/badge_browser.py` checks it with a generated test image.

Cinematic intro: a 31-second film rendered in the game's 3D engine opens the game (skippable; "Tap for sound" for the synthesised soundtrack): a night fly-over of a bowl stadium inspired by a famous North London ground (no names or logos; also Arsenal's home ground in the 3D view), the walk-out, title cards, a dribble, a low-angle goal with the net rippling, a corner celebration with flares and confetti, and the final title. Game > Options turns it on or off, plays it again, and lets you add your own "Intro photo" with a caption: the photo is kept in this browser only (IndexedDB) and never uploaded or published. Without an internet connection the 2D intro cards play instead.

Manager statue: reaching legend status at a club (a league title and five seasons, or three major trophies) unveils an illustrated bronze statue outside the ground: the head is your own manager avatar (hair, beard, glasses) cast in bronze, the pose follows your honours (trophy aloft, fist pump or pointing), and a plaque gives your name, years, trophies and games. Eight trophies at the club add a gold laurel and "Club legend". The statue appears on the day (with the club-coloured cloth sliding off), on the club's honours page and on your manager profile.

Former players on the staff: Staff lists up to ten retired players and veterans in their last contract year (your own 33+ players included) who would start a new career as First-Team Coach, Assistant Manager, Chief Scout or Head Physio. Each has a non-playing profile (also reachable from his player card): ability for every role, coaching badges, medical training, speciality by position, scouting contacts, personality and his playing career in the save. Hiring one ends his playing career (a club still holding his contract asks a release fee); well-known players sometimes retire into a staff job at their last club on their own.

National-team trophies (World Cup, European Championship, Nations League) get a full celebration: a long news story (final score, how many titles and since when, top scorer, your club's players among the winners), a victory parade through the capital with a drawn parade scene, a drawn trophy banner in the inbox, a popup when it is your nation, "International honours" on the winners' player cards, a national honour and trophy banners on the manager profile if you managed them, and a trophy cabinet on International › History. Trophy drawings are generic shapes, not the real designs.

## Layout

- `src/`: game source (data, match engine, season, transfers, UI, 2D matchday, awards, manager profile, styles)
- `build.py`: joins `src/` into `gaffer.html` (and `bundle.js` for tests)
- `gaffer.html`: the latest built game
- `tests/`: Node simulation tests and Playwright smoke tests (run from the repo root after building), e.g. `node tests/ch_test.js WHU 2`
- `tools/build_ch.py`: builds `src/data_ch.js` (Championship squads) from the raw lists in `tools/ch_raw/` (FotMob team pages, 24-28 Oct 2026); ratings are game estimates
- `tools/build_youth.py`: builds `src/data_youth.js` (real reserve and academy players for 28 clubs) from `tools/youth_raw/` (FotMob U21 team pages, 28-29 Oct 2026); clubs without a published list get generated youth players marked GEN
- `tools/build_hist.py`: builds `src/data_hist.js` (real winners and runners-up to 2025/26) from `tools/hist_raw/` (Wikipedia list pages, read 30 Oct 2026)
- `src/data_stadia.js`: stadium facts (opening year, record crowd, pitch size, stand names) from Wikipedia stadium pages; `src/data_mgr.js`: managers and major honours for the world rankings
- `tools/build_ll.py`: builds `src/data_ll.js` (La Liga squads, Segunda and Primera Federación sides, Spanish stadium facts, Spain caps) from `tools/ll_raw/` (FotMob team pages, 31 Oct 2026; coaches, captains and grounds from Wikipedia "2026-27 La Liga"); ages, positions and ratings are game estimates, Spanish reserve and academy players are generated (GEN)
- `src/selection.js`: Best XI / Rotate / Youth XI and saved tactics on the Tactics screen
- `src/social.js`: Media screen (fans, fictional reporters, rumours, trending)
- `src/clublife.js`: player requests with answers, documentary, revived Cup Winners' Cup (game scenario) and Club World Cup (game format), trophy room, trophy parade, statue, manager's son, save export/import as a file
- `src/banter.js`: pundits, creators, blame and banter threads, VAR Watch
- `src/scenario.js`: optional storylines (Manchester City sanction)
- `src/laliga.js`: Spanish competitions, Copa del Rey and Supercopa draws, season end, migration that adds Spain to older saves
- `src/data_intl.js`: nations (FIFA ranking, Oct 2026), Nations League 2026/27 draw and results, England caps, international winners; checked 31 Oct 2026
- `src/rivalry.js`, `src/preseason.js`, `src/polish.js`: rivalry banter, sponsors and news write-ups; pre-season, Asia Tour and Arsenal Legends; grouped navigation, inbox digest and filters, tips, cup and calibration tweaks
- `tests/run_all.sh [OUT_DIR] [JOBS]`: builds and runs every node and browser test, prints ok/FAIL per test; `tests/calib.js`: 10-season calibration run
- `docs/screenshots/`: reference screenshots

## Build

```bash
python3 build.py      # writes gaffer.html + bundle.js
python3 build_pwa.py  # writes the web app: index.html, manifest, sw.js, icons/
```

Saves are compressed in browser storage (LZW, about a tenth of the JSON size); older uncompressed saves still load. Export backup gives plain JSON.

`src/vis.js` is an older draft of the 2D view and is not part of the build.
